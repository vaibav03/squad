# Claude Design prompt: Squad Campus (top-down interior world)

Attach: your Modern Interiors reference image (the cozy classroom), your 4-image collage (the pixel office is the second most relevant), Nook screenshots and styles.css, design-checklist.md. Licensing note: the Modern Interiors license allows use and editing in your own projects but forbids passing the asset files to others, so attach screenshots of the look, not the pack itself; Claude Design should draw placeholder tiles in the same style and the real tiles are swapped in by the build.

R: Here are my references: a screenshot of LimeZu's Modern Interiors style (a cozy top-down classroom: tiled floor, desks, shelves, play mats, round tables, plants, a chalkboard, small characters, walls and doorways between rooms) and a collage of game scenes (the pixel office where AI characters walk between rooms is the closest). Here is my app: screenshots and styles.css of a student-assistant web app, plus design-checklist.md. The product lives in students' iMessage; the web app is where a student goes to see their study agents and join them.

I: Design "Squad Campus", a walkable top-down pixel-art indoor campus that is the front door to every agent in my app. A student walks their character from room to room; each room is an agent or a council they can enter. Before designing, write DESIGN.md from my screenshots and the references: pixel scale (16px tiles drawn at an integer zoom), palette, lighting, HUD style, type, and motion rules. Show me 2 directions for the Commons (the main hub room) only, then wait for my pick. After I pick, design these, desktop (1280px) first, then phone (390px):

1. The campus map and Commons: a floor plan of connected rooms around a central Commons with a big shared table, plants, a notice board and a reception desk. The student's own character walks in four directions (arrow keys or WASD, tap-to-walk with pathfinding on touch). Top HUD: small portrait chips for every agent showing its state (idle, studying, in a call, waiting for you), a minimap of the floor plan with a dot per agent, a "Calm mode" toggle (fewer, gentler animations), and a clock. A collapsible side panel in the style of my web app lists the same agents.
2. Chapter classrooms (the swarm of experts): one classroom per chapter off a corridor, each with a door sign (chapter name), desks, a chalkboard, and a lamp that is lit when its expert is awake. A mastery glow on the door frame (dim to bright) shows how well the student knows the chapter. Walking in shows the room with the expert at the teacher's desk.
3. The Council Room (LLM council): a round room with 3 council members at a circular table plus a chairman's chair. Asking a question makes each member's thought bubble appear independently, then speech bubbles pass between them to rank each other, then the chairman announces the verdict. Show the dissenting view as a lone red-lined bubble. Disagreement is shown as a flickering lamp ("uncertain, ask your TA").
4. Other council rooms, each distinct: Debate Hall (a small lecture hall, two experts at lecterns, the group votes with colored cards), Course-Planning Room (chill agent, study-hard agent, balance agent around a table with a map of next semester), Feynman Corner (a confused freshman NPC on a couch asking naive "why"s), Panic Line (an infirmary-style room whose lamp glows at 2am), Oral Exam Room (a strict examiner and a single chair), Rubric Room, Exam Oracle (the library's reading room), Devil's TA (a messy back office with a mistake hidden on a chalkboard). Locked or dimmed until relevant.
5. Join the call: walking up to an agent and pressing "Join call" opens a call panel over the scene: the agent's pixel portrait animates while it talks, a live voice waveform, running captions with citations ("[slides.pdf p.12]"), mute, hand-raise, and leave. The group version shows several students standing around the same table.
6. Quiz rounds: stepping into a classroom starts a quiz as an interior scene: question on the chalkboard, plain answer choices, instant feedback. No bets, no scores, no streaks, and never show grades.
7. The agent mesh view: each neighbour student's private room along a side corridor has a locked door (private). When agents negotiate, they walk out to the Commons table carrying envelopes; speech bubbles show only shared fields; a conflict shows a red "!"; an agent waiting for its student's yes stands at its own door with a pulsing ring; the notice board at reception is the disclosure ledger (shared vs withheld). When every student has confirmed the plan, a trip cutscene plays once and is skippable: the camera pans to the lobby windows, a campus shuttle pulls up outside and drives off, and the Trip door opens (no boss, no gate; trip planning unlocks on everyone's confirmation).
8. Replay mode: a "Demo replay" toggle plays a scripted version of events (same visuals) so the demo never depends on live timing. A small badge says "Replay" vs "Live".
9. Mobile: the campus shows one room at a time with a virtual joystick or tap-to-walk, a bottom dock of agent chips, and a minimap sheet; joining a call or quiz opens a full-screen sheet.

S: Style: cozy top-down pixel art in the spirit of Modern Interiors, at one consistent scale (16px tiles, whole-pixel positioning, no blurry scaling: image-rendering: pixelated), soft warm interior light with pools of lamp light and a gentle shadow under furniture, characters y-sorted behind and in front of furniture. The campus is the scene; panels, buttons and text around it follow my existing web app's tokens so the two feel like one product. Draw placeholder tiles and sprites in this style (the real tileset is swapped in during the build), never another brand's logo or characters. Privacy is part of the design: never show grades, budgets or who is behind; show team progress. Every screen has designed empty, loading, error and "agent offline" states. Plain, friendly student-voice copy; no jargon like "MoE", "PSI" or "mesh". Credits line for all art (LimeZu, Modern Interiors).

MOTION (follow docs/specs/08-village-motion.md; adapt it to top-down): gate every animation by frequency and purpose (feedback, spatial consistency, state indication, explanation; delight only for rare moments like the trip cutscene). Walking is a 100+/day action, so it is never eased: constant speed, instant start, 80ms ease-out stop, whole pixels, no camera lag on key input. NPC agents walk along pathfinding routes at the same speed. Define tokens once in DESIGN.md: --ease-out: cubic-bezier(0.23, 1, 0.32, 1); --ease-in-out: cubic-bezier(0.77, 0, 0.175, 1); --ease-drawer: cubic-bezier(0.32, 0.72, 0, 1). No ease-in, no other curves. UI under 300ms; world animations (walk cycles, bubbles, envelopes, camera pans of about 400ms) may run longer. Panels and HUD animate transform and opacity only; sprite animation uses spritesheet frames. Words never move for style: captions, ledger text, quiz questions and bubbles appear whole and stay at least 1.2s per 20 characters; withheld text is a static black bar. Idle loops pause when the tab is hidden. Nothing flashes more than 3 times a second. Every animation has a prefers-reduced-motion variant (ambient loops stop, walking becomes teleport-with-fade, bubbles fade without movement, the cutscene becomes a static card). Deliver a motion table in DESIGN.md.

E: Before you call it done, run design-checklist.md with 5 read-only subagents (layout and phone, type and rhythm, colour and states, motion and access, message and action), plus a sixth read-only motion reviewer against the MOTION rules. Check at 390px and 1280px, keyboard-only play (arrow keys to walk, Enter to enter or join, Esc to leave), a text alternative for every room so a screen reader can reach every agent, and flag dead buttons and placeholder text. Merge into one fix list and show me. Change nothing until I say go. Then fix it yourself and show before and after screenshots.
