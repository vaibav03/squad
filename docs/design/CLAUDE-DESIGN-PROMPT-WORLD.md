I: Design "Squad Village", a walkable side-view pixel-art world that is the front door to every agent in my app. A student walks their character through the village; each building is an agent or a council they can enter. Before designing, write DESIGN.md from my screenshots and the references: pixel scale, palette, night and day lighting, HUD style, type, and motion rules. Show me 2 directions for the main village scene only, then wait for my pick. After I pick, design these, desktop (1280px) first, then phone (390px):

1. The village (main scene): side-scrolling parallax at night (starry sky, moon, clouds, rolling hills, trees, a windmill, lit windows, chimney smoke, fence). The student's own character walks left and right, with tap-to-walk on touch. Top HUD: small portrait chips for every agent showing its state (idle, studying, in a call, waiting for you), camera buttons (Left / Mid / Right / Zoom), a "Calm mode" toggle (fewer, gentler animations) and the day/night clock that follows the real time. Collapsible side panel in the style of my web app listing the same agents.
2. Chapter houses (the swarm of experts): one cozy house per chapter, in a row along the hill. Each house has a signboard (chapter name), a lit window when its expert is awake, and a mastery glow (dim → bright) for how well the student knows it. Walking in opens the house interior as a cutaway room with the expert at a desk.
3. The Council Hall (LLM council): a round hall with 3 council members at a circular table plus a chairman's seat. Asking a question makes each member's thought bubble appear independently, then speech bubbles pass between them to rank each other, then the chairman announces the verdict. Show the dissenting view as a lone red-lined bubble. Disagreement between members is shown as a flickering lantern ("uncertain, ask your TA").
4. Other council buildings, each a distinct landmark: Debate Stage (two experts at lecterns, the group votes with lanterns), Course-Planning Round Table (chill agent, study-hard agent, balance agent around a map of next semester), Feynman Porch (a confused freshman NPC on a porch asking naive "why"s), Panic Line phone booth (glows at 2am), Oral Exam Hut (a strict examiner and a single chair), Rubric Courthouse, Exam Oracle observatory, Devil's TA cave (a mischievous NPC hiding a mistake in a chalkboard). Locked or dimmed until relevant.
5. Join the call: walking up to an agent and pressing "Join call" opens a call panel over the scene: the agent's pixel portrait animates while it talks, a live voice waveform, running captions with citations ("[slides.pdf p.12]"), mute, hand-raise, and leave. The group version shows several students as characters standing in the same room.
6. Quiz rounds: stepping into a classroom house starts a quiz as an interior scene: question card on the chalkboard, plain answer choices and instant feedback. No bets, scores or streaks, and never show grades.
7. The agent mesh view: neighbour students' houses appear along the hill with locked doors (private). When agents negotiate, they walk out to a campfire commons carrying envelopes; speech bubbles show only shared fields; a conflict shows a red "!"; an agent waiting for its student's yes stands at its door with a pulsing ring; the notice board at the commons is the disclosure ledger (shared vs withheld). When every student has confirmed the plan, the trip cutscene plays once and is skippable: the road scrolls, a vehicle drives off, and the Trip door pops open (no boss, no gate; trip planning unlocks on everyone's confirmation).
8. Replay mode: a "Demo replay" toggle plays a scripted version of events (same visuals) so the demo never depends on live timing. A small badge says "Replay" vs "Live".
9. Mobile: the village becomes a single horizontal scroll strip with a bottom dock of agent chips; joining a call or quiz opens a full-screen sheet.

S: Style: cozy pixel art at one consistent scale (16px tiles, whole-pixel positioning, no blurry scaling: image-rendering: pixelated), night palette with warm window light, soft glow for magic, in the spirit of the side-view night reference. The village is the scene; panels, buttons and text around it follow my existing web app's tokens so the two feel like one product. Never use another brand's logo or characters. Use the attached pixel assets for the scene (backgrounds, houses, characters, effects) and the attached 3D set only for small UI chrome. Privacy is part of the design: never show grades, budgets or who is behind; show team progress. Every screen has designed empty, loading, error and "agent offline" states. Plain, friendly student-voice copy; no jargon like "MoE", "PSI" or "mesh". Credits line for all art.

MOTION (follow 08-village-motion.md, the single motion plan; follow it exactly):
- Gate first: every animation gets a frequency (100+/day, tens/day, occasional, rare) and one purpose (feedback, spatial consistency, state indication, preventing a jarring change, explanation, delight). No purpose, no animation.
- Walking is the game, so it is never eased: constant speed, instant start, 80ms ease-out stop, whole pixels, no camera lag on key input. Delight only in rare moments (the trip cutscene, first arrival in the village).
- Words never move for style: captions, the notice-board ledger, quiz questions and bubbles appear whole and stay at least 1.2s per 20 characters; withheld text is a static black bar. Quiz answers and feedback change colour only (150ms). Nothing repeats faster than once a second except sprite frames. Nothing is a score.
- Tokens, defined once in DESIGN.md and shared by canvas and DOM, no other curves, never ease-in: --ease-out: cubic-bezier(0.23, 1, 0.32, 1) (enter/exit); --ease-in-out: cubic-bezier(0.77, 0, 0.175, 1) (moving on screen); --ease-drawer: cubic-bezier(0.32, 0.72, 0, 1) (panels from an edge). Rare-delight spring only: { duration: 0.5, bounce: 0.2 }.
- Tools: canvas uses spritesheet frames plus tweens on transform and alpha (whole-pixel sprite positions, pixelArt, one atlas per scene, 60fps on a phone). DOM overlays (call, quiz, ledger, HUD) animate only transform and opacity (clip-path for the check draw) with CSS transitions and @starting-style; never transition: all, never scale(0) (enter from scale(0.95) plus opacity 0). UI under 300ms (panels 200-280ms); world animations (bubbles, council sequence, envelopes, camera pans of about 400ms) may run longer. Hover only in (hover: hover) and (pointer: fine).
- Pause the game loop when the tab is hidden and resume without catching up. Nothing flashes more than 3 times a second; no autoplay audio.
- Reduced motion: every animation has a prefers-reduced-motion variant, fewer and gentler, not zero (parallax and ambient loops stop, walking becomes teleport-with-fade, bubbles fade without movement, trip cutscene becomes a static card). The "Calm mode" toggle in the HUD applies the same variants.
- Deliver a motion table in DESIGN.md: animation, where (canvas or DOM), frequency, purpose, tool, curve and time, reduced-motion variant.
